Informed Consent

Study Title:

Effects of Performance Pressure and Task Difficulty on Problem-Solving

Principal Investigator:

Aidan Barry – Psych 402 Experimental Research, Saint Mary's College of California

Purpose:

This study investigates how performance pressure and task difficulty affect problem-solving ability using the Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

Procedures:

You will complete a short puzzle (estimated time: 1–6 minutes). Your completion time and number of moves will be recorded automatically. The task is done entirely online.

Risks and Benefits:

Risks are minimal (possible mild frustration). There are no direct personal benefits, but your participation helps advance psychological research.

Confidentiality & Voluntary Participation:

Participation is voluntary and anonymous. You may withdraw at any time without penalty by closing the page. No identifying information is collected.

Instructions

Solve the Tower of Hanoi

Moves: 0

Debriefing

This study examined the effects of performance pressure and task difficulty on problem-solving performance.

Manipulations:

  • Pressure: Some participants received a high-pressure prompt (“This is a test of intelligence…”), while others received neutral instructions.
  • Difficulty: You were randomly assigned to either the 3-disk (easier) or 4-disk (harder) version.

Your data (completion time and moves) have been recorded anonymously.

This research is inspired by classic studies on “choking under pressure” (e.g., Baumeister, 1984; Beilock & Carr, 2005).
