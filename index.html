Study Title:

Effects of Performance Pressure and Task Difficulty on Problem-Solving Performance

Principal Investigator:

Aidan Barry - Psych 402 Experimental Research, Saint Mary's College of California

Purpose of the Study:

This study is investigating how performance pressure and task difficulty influence problem-solving ability using the Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

Procedures:

If you agree to participate, you will complete the Tower of Hanoi puzzle on your computer. The task should take between 1 and 6 minutes. Your completion time and number of moves will be automatically recorded. The entire process should take no longer than 10 minutes.

Risks and Discomforts:

There are minimal risks associated with this study. You may experience mild frustration if the puzzle is challenging, similar to any cognitive task.

Benefits:

There are no direct benefits to participants. However, your participation will contribute to our understanding of cognitive performance and will help fulfill course requirements for Psych 402.

Confidentiality:

All data collected is anonymous. No identifying information (such as your name or email) will be collected or stored.

Voluntary Participation and Right to Withdraw:

Your participation is completely voluntary. You may refuse to participate or withdraw at any time without penalty by simply closing the browser window.

Debriefing Information

Thank you for your participation in this study. The purpose of this research was to examine the effects of performance pressure and task difficulty on problem-solving performance using the Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

Manipulations Used:

  • Performance Pressure: Participants were randomly assigned to either a neutral condition or a pressure condition. The pressure condition included the prompt: “This is a test of intelligence. Many people struggle with this task...”
  • Task Difficulty: Participants were randomly assigned to either a 3-disk version (easier) or a 4-disk version (harder).

Hypotheses:
We predicted that pressure would increase completion time, especially on the more difficult (4-disk) version.

This study was inspired by previous research including Baumeister (1984) on choking under pressure and Beilock & Carr (2005) on how pressure affects working memory during problem-solving tasks.

Your data has been recorded anonymously. If you wish to withdraw your data, please contact Aidan Barry at adb15@stmarys-ca.edu as soon as possible.
